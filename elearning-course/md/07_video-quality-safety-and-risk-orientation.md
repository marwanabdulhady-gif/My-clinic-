## 1. What this source is

A **general orientation video for new clinical staff** on how My Clinic runs **clinical governance**: quality standards, patient-safety goals, risk management, and incident reporting. It is presented as the "official general orientation session" and is designed as a single narrated walkthrough (a slide-style presentation with NotebookLM-style animated visuals — the frames carry a *NotebookLM* watermark, which should be checked against the organisation's licence before reuse).

### Five-part structure (as stated in the narration at ~01:14)
1. Defining the **quality standard**
2. **Patient-safety goals** (six International Patient Safety Goals)
3. **Risk management**
4. **Incident events** (the critical-incident iceberg)
5. The **incident reporting workflow**

### Learning objectives (derived from the content)
By the end of the module, learners should be able to:
1. Describe the **Structure → Processes → Outcomes** quality model and the **FOCUS-PDCA** improvement cycle.
2. Name the **six patient-safety goals** and apply the **two patient identifiers** rule.
3. Use the **risk-scoring matrix** (impact × likelihood, 1–25) and the four risk responses.
4. Distinguish critical incidents, adverse events and near misses.
5. Follow the **24-hour incident-reporting** requirement.

## 3. Cleaned narration (working draft)

> **Read this as a draft.** The machine transcript (below) has word errors, and the cleaned version corrects only what is clear from the slides and context. Re-listen to the video before using any quoted sentence in assessments.

**[00:00] Welcome.** Welcome to all the new clinical staff joining us. Today we go behind the scenes into the core of how My Clinic operates. Our overarching goal is simple, and vital: learn how to build the protection of our patients through clinical governance. This is the official general orientation session for My Clinic staff, so these visuals are the real-world blueprint for your daily work.

**[00:44] Quality standard.** The orientation starts with one foundational quote: *doing the right things right, the first time and every time.* It establishes that excellence is the baseline expectation here, not a vague aspiration. We aim for precision from the start.

**[01:14] Agenda.** Five parts: the quality standard, patient-safety goals, risk management, incident events, and the reporting workflow.

**[01:26] Section 1 — the quality standard.** Quality is measured from left to right: *structure* (the physical and logistical resources to deliver care), *processes* (how well the team performs the activities), and *outcomes* (the actual results). The big takeaway: collect valid, reliable data that reflects our actual performance; your resources and your actions together produce your patient outcomes. At the centre is the **FOCUS-PDCA** model, the officially approved quality-improvement cycle at My Clinic: find a problem, plan, do, check the results, act on them. It keeps us moving forward.

**[02:48] Section 2 — the six patient-safety pillars.** Also called the International Patient Safety Goals (IPSG). They cover correct identification, effective communication, high-alert medications, safe surgery, hand hygiene and fall prevention.
- **Goal 1 — identification:** use only the two official identifiers: the patient's full name and medical record number. *Never use age, room number or anything else as an identifier.*
- **Goal 2 — communication:** strict protocols, including verifying verbal and bulk orders, reporting critical diagnostic results quickly, and structured handover at shift change.

**[03:45] Goals 3–6.**
- **Goal 3 — high-alert medications:** a red stop-sign warning; a strict process and a red label; zero room for error.
- **Goal 4 — surgical safety:** pre-procedure verification, marking the surgical site, the **time-out** (the whole team stops and verifies immediately before starting), and the **sign-out** before the patient leaves the room.
- **Goal 5 — hand hygiene:** the five critical moments — before touching a patient, before a procedure, after body-fluid exposure, after touching a patient, and after touching the patient's surroundings.
- **Goal 6 — fall prevention:** high-risk patients wear a bright-yellow identifier band.
- Hand hygiene and fall prevention are daily non-negotiables.

**[03:45] Section 3 — risk management.** A lifecycle: identify the risk; assess it; manage (put controls in place); monitor the controls; report the exposure so it is recorded in the **risk register**. It feeds the clinic's early-warning system.
- **Scoring:** use the corporate colour matrix. Read the impact (1–5) on one axis and the likelihood (1–5) on the other; multiply. 1 × 1 = 1 is very low (green). 5 × 5 = 25 is very high (deep red). Everyone uses the same language.
- **Responses:** control it (reduce impact or likelihood), transfer it (e.g. to insurance), accept it (only with executive review and approval), or avoid it (do not start the activity).

**[06:25] Section 4 — incident events.** Imagine an iceberg. The tip is the **critical incident** (fatalities, catastrophic impacts). Just below the surface: incidents with adverse impact. Underwater: **near misses** — failures detected and prevented before harm. My Clinic has an open, non-punitive culture; our safety depends on reporting those near misses without fear of blame, so we can fix the system before a critical incident happens.
- **Critical incident** = unanticipated event involving death or serious injury that signals a need for investigation. Examples: fatality, wrong-site surgery, infant abduction, workplace violence. These demand an immediate, system-wide leadership response.

**[07:30] Section 5 — the incident-reporting workflow.** Safety covers clinical care (medication errors, falls) and facility matters (system downtime, equipment failure, lab issues, fire hazards, security breaches). Safety is everyone's job.
- **Step 1 — the 24-hour rule:** every employee has **24 hours** to submit a digital report (via the internet system or email) when they witness an incident. The clock starts when you witness it. Once you submit, the system takes over: grading, escalation, and analysis where needed. If you do not submit, the system does not start.

**[08:35] Closing.** You are the final and most essential layer of our protection. *When you see something, say something.* Welcome to the team, and think about how you will make an impact on patient safety.
