# Client context — My Clinic KSA (عيادتي)

**Internal project brief · researched 14 October 2026**

## Identity and positioning

The client in this repository is **My Clinic / My Clinic KSA (عيادتي)**, a Saudi Arabian multispecialty outpatient-care organization operating in Jeddah and Riyadh. The official Arabic-language “About Us” page says the organization was established in 2017 under the stewardship of Nazer Group. It describes its purpose as helping people live longer, healthier and happier lives and its mission as becoming the world's most customer-focused healthcare company. It lists four core values: caring, ambition, responsibility and collaboration. [1]

The official page positions the organization around preventive care, diagnostics, specialist treatment and a patient-centred experience. The current public site names specialties including family medicine, cardiology, paediatrics, women's health, dentistry, dermatology, internal medicine, radiology, laboratory services, pharmacy, physiotherapy, home care and personal-care coordinators. [1] [2]

The official site currently lists six locations: Al Mohammadiyah (main centre), Al Safa, a dental centre in Al Khalidiyyah, Al Tahlia and Obhour in Jeddah, and a main centre in Riyadh/Al Sahafa. [1] [2] Use the live locations page for operational details; do not copy branch hours, service availability or addresses into training without client approval because those can change.

## Scale and public claims

The organization's official About page reports **more than 24 specialties** and **more than 300 healthcare professionals**. The same site's home-page footer instead says **more than 27 specialties and 100+ doctors**. These are not directly equivalent measures, but the numbers are inconsistent enough that course materials and proposals should avoid a single workforce or specialty count until My Clinic confirms an approved current figure. [1] [2]

The official site says its centres are CBAHI accredited. The repository's course content also refers to CBAHI and other regulators, but accreditation wording, certificate scope and current validity should be confirmed by the client before it is used as a course claim. [1]

Apple's current Saudi App Store listing identifies the developer as **MY CLINIC INTERNATIONAL MEDICAL CO LTD**. It describes appointment booking, online doctor consultations, pharmacy queueing, booking history, promotions and feedback, and lists English and Arabic as app languages. This supports the digital-service context but does not replace an approved policy or workflow source. [3]

## Operating model and technology context

A 2017 AGFA HealthCare customer story describes My Clinic's Jeddah site as a pilot/flagship for an outpatient-clinic network, with a goal of combining clinical quality with a strong patient experience. It discusses a broad outpatient service mix and a centralized imaging platform intended to make imaging information available to care teams across locations. AGFA is the solution vendor, so these are vendor-published descriptions, not independent performance findings. The story also says the first Jeddah clinic opened in May 2017. Its future expansion references are historical and must not be repeated as current plans. [4]

A GE HealthCare case study describes My Clinic's cardiology use of Centricity Cardio Enterprise. It reports that My Clinic's Cardiology Department supplied data indicating a 40% increase in Echo patient capacity and a reduction in examination time from one hour to 25 minutes. Those are vendor-case-study results attributed to My Clinic and dated to data supplied as of May 2023; they should be treated as attributed historical claims, not current audited measures. [5]

A 2020 CFI.co award profile describes My Clinic's outpatient and home-care offering, including consultations, sample collection, vaccinations, physiotherapy, post-surgery care and elderly home care. It also reports corporate-wellness services and names My Clinic as the recipient of its 2020 “Best Clinical Patient Outcome (KSA)” award. This is useful context about historic public positioning, not evidence of present-day outcomes or current accreditation. [6]

## What this means for the induction programme

The course plans are directionally consistent with the public profile: a multi-specialty, multi-branch outpatient provider needs consistent onboarding across clinical and non-clinical teams; safe patient identification and documentation; coordinated front-office, clinical and My Care workflows; and privacy-safe practice in software simulations. The official public positioning also makes empathy, clear communication, reliable service and collaboration appropriate tone anchors for the course. These are design implications, not additional client-approved policies. [1] [4]

The project source folder and its clinical/HR/RCM materials remain the authority for course facts. Public research should only supply organizational context. It must **not** be used to resolve open Phase 0 policy, emergency-code, regulatory, payer, HR, branch-structure, EHR or quality decisions.

### Current approval questions to resolve with My Clinic

- Confirm one approved public description and a current count for specialties, clinicians and locations; reconcile the official site's different “24+/300+” and “27+/100+” statements.
- Confirm what CBAHI accreditation statement may be used, and its precise scope and current validity.
- Confirm the current Nazer Group relationship and preferred legal/brand name for the proposal, learner screens and metadata.
- Approve which branch names and organizational labels are safe to include. The source materials contain inconsistencies in branch naming and dates.
- Confirm which digital tools, screenshots and service details remain current. The project already identifies personally identifiable patient data in the committed original IT video; do not use it as a visual source. Use a DPO-approved fictional test patient and fresh test-environment captures only.
- Approve the brand assets and final voice identities through the open D11 and D10 decisions. Do not infer voice identity from public videos or employee speech.
