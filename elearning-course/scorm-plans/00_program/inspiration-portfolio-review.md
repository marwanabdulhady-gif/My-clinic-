## 2. Per-project findings (pages read only)

### Sabrina Gonzales — Walk the Walk (sabrinaglxd.com/walk-the-walk)
- **Problem and audience.** New dog-walker volunteers and staff at a conceptual shelter. Their walks went badly from a mix of skill and knowledge gaps: they "are not aware of the behaviors to look for, do not know when to reward".
- **Tool.** Storyline 360 and Figma, using the ADDIE process.
- **Mechanics.**
  - Choose-your-avatar: the learner's name appears throughout.
  - Each decision has one correct option and two distractors.
  - A **success meter**: "implemented as a slider that moved based on the choices made", showing the learner "they could be helping Milo get closer to adoption".
- **Help on demand.** A job-aid button with "subtle hints", and a **mentor button** with advice from a veteran walker. Both are introduced with animation so learners find them.
- **Feedback.** A right choice shows a positive consequence. A wrong choice shows a negative consequence "of varying severity", and the learner can retry.
- **Why it isn't boring.** The rules ("loose leash, four paws down, no yelling") are never listed as rules. You only meet them through what happens to the dog.

### Sabrina Gonzales — When Disaster Strikes: Are You Prepared? (sabrinaglxd.com/when-disaster-strikes)
- **Audience.** Healthcare professionals in post-acute care who must make split-second emergency decisions.
- **Tool.** Storyline 360, Figma, and PowerPoint for animations exported as MP4. Sound effects were matched to the storyboard.
- **Feedback design.** Question, then options, then a consequence for each option. The designer chose to *show* what happens after a choice rather than tell the learner. Custom animation and sound are used for immersion.

### Steven Chea — Health & Safety (stevenchea.com/portfolio/health-safety)
- **Audience.** Staff of a New Zealand healthcare provider, trained on procedures under the Health and Safety in Employment Act.
- **Tool.** Storyline, built from a PowerPoint storyboard.
- **Mechanics.**
  - **Role picker at the start.** "The learner is prompted to specify their role at the beginning."
  - "Several paths cover the same content within department-appropriate scenarios."
  - The course reacts to each answer and to the overall score through variables and triggers.
- **Feedback.** Feedback is used "to build knowledge even when a learner answers incorrectly".
- **Lesson for us.** This is the closest match to our role-picker plan: the same objectives in role-flavoured scenarios.

### Steven Chea — Asbestos Training (stevenchea.com/portfolio/asbestos-training)
- **Audience.** Facilities engineers at a UK research facility, working to HSE standards.
- **Tool.** Storyline, Word, and WellSaid Labs AI voice.
- **Structure.**
  - Each section ends with an ungraded knowledge check.
  - A final graded test needs 80% to pass.
  - On a wrong answer the learner retries. On the third wrong attempt the correct answer is shown.
- **Review table (client request).** After a failed test, a table "summarizes the outcome of each response and names the section to review for each missed question".
- **Visuals.** **Each section has its own colour theme**, with flip cards and click-and-reveal scenarios.

### Steven Chea — The Customer Connection (stevenchea.com/portfolio/the-customer-connection)
- **Audience.** New IT customer-support staff and staff who struggle with difficult customer emails.
- **Mechanics.**
  - Five scenarios built from **real customer emails**.
  - Each has one ideal reply and two flawed replies.
  - Each choice triggers a **realistic customer reply**, which shows whether the choice worked and adds emotional weight.
- **Trainer character.** A trainer called Vernon "provides hints and feedback that can be accessed at the learner's discretion".
- **Scoring.** 1 to 5 stars per run.
- **Results claimed.**
  - In a pilot with associates in their 4th week, the first-attempt average was 4.2 out of 5 stars (pass mark 4).
  - One associate kept the checklist at her workstation.
  - The SME said participants were "more confident in solving problems independently".
  - The sample was small and the results are self-reported.

### Kassie Calvo — Can You Handle a Day in the Lab? (kassiedesigns.webflow.io/projects/lab-project)
- **Problem.** Staff were mishandling chemical storage and disposal, which caused injuries and damaged equipment.
- **Audience.** Chemists, lab technicians and lab managers.
- **Tools.** Storyline 360, Illustrator and Adobe XD.
- **Analysis with the SME.**
  - She and a lab-manager SME mapped the tasks most often done wrong and focused on the two with "the direst consequences": storage and disposal.
  - Labeling went to a **job aid** instead of the course.
- **Structure.**
  - A "typical lab day" story with five pain points: lunch storage, unattended experiments, returning chemicals, disposal and cleanup.
  - The scenes are built from the SME's real stories, for example "boiling over, decomposition, or localized explosions".
- **Mechanics.** A custom **drag-and-drop storeroom**: the learner drops each chemical where they think it belongs. Variables record each placement, and "the variable for each chemical was totaled upon submission" to give feedback per item.

### Melanie Adkison — Take Flight (melanielxd.com/scenario-based-elearning)
- **Problem and audience.** Newly hired ticket agents at a fictitious airline with rising negative reviews. Action mapping showed new agents "struggled with identifying and resolving problems" and "were not applying simple strategies to catch mistakes".
- **Tools.** Storyline 360, Illustrator, Figma and MindMeister, using Cathy Moore's Map It and Mayer's principles.
- **Structure.** Three modules (ticket, gate, baggage). The demo is the ticket module.
- **Design choice.** **Narration instead of on-screen text** (the modality principle), to build empathy for passengers.
- **Not visible.** The mechanics and visuals of the demo itself were not in the index.

### Jeremy Chestnut — Transmission-Based Precautions (jeremychestnutlxd.com/flagship-writeup; peckacademy.com/showcase/jeremy-chestnut)
- **Problem and audience.** At a fictional health centre, an outbreak caused staff shortages and longer patient stays. The infection-control team concluded it could have been prevented with proper precautions. Learners are doctors, nurses and support staff.
- **Tools.** Storyline 360 and Vyond, using action mapping and Social Learning Theory.
- **Why a course, not only a job aid.** The write-up proposed a job aid but said it would not change behaviour on its own, because staff rarely see these cases.
- **Mechanics.**
  - Character-driven decision points.
  - A **mentor character**.
  - "A subtle visual hint system that helps learners identify the correct actions after making mistakes."
- **Visuals.**
  - "Subtle colors commonly associated with hospital settings, creating a professional and calming look."
  - Text colour pairs were checked for WCAG contrast.
  - Hover states on clickable items and on the mentor.

### Catherine Schwebel — Medication Management for Nurses (catherineschwebel.com/featured-project)
- **Problem and audience.** Nurses across five sites of a conceptual care home. Errors cluster around **timing, route and measurement accuracy**, traced to a skills gap and protocol not being followed.
- **Why e-learning.** Many sites and shift patterns.
- **Tools.** Storyline 360, Figma, MindMeister and ChatGPT. She worked with a nurse SME.
- **Measurable goal stated up front.** A 15% fall in medication errors within six months (target only; no result given).
- **Structure.** Branching that "reinforces decision-making and accountability" (Peck Academy summary).

### Rachna Ghiya — Boring Compliance to Decision-Driven eLearning, Radiometer (rachnaghiya.com)
- **Problem.** Mandatory training for nurses, framed as "Imagine a nurse, juggling multiple tasks…". Passive content was replaced with **14 decision-based scenarios**.
- **Design steps (page headings).** "relatable characters" → scenario → "outcome-based-response" → "showing the correct response" → "reiterating the learning".
- **Delivery.** English and German. A self-paced reference resource for trainers is also mentioned.
- **Results.** None found.

### Nan Wang — Multicultural Classroom Management (nanwanglxd.com/projects/multicultural-classroom-management)
- **Audience.** English-language instructors.
- **Structure.** One conflict story with **three decision points × three actions**. Each action has different consequences for "students, classroom dynamics, and the instructor".
- **Feedback.** Immediate, in a "low-stakes environment". A **mentor character** gives on-demand help.
- **Other details.**
  - A **trigger warning** is shown because the topic is sensitive.
  - Prototype feedback led to characters being placed **facing each other** to make the conflict clearer.

## 3. Patterns that recur across the best projects

1. **The title is a "Can you…?" challenge, not a topic name.** Examples: "Can You Handle a Day in the Lab?", "Can You Keep Your Crew Safe?", "Can you effectively prevent a patient fall?", "Can you protect client data?", "When Disaster Strikes: Are You Prepared?". We already do this.

2. **"A day in the job" as the story spine.** Calvo's lab day (5 pain points in time order), Campo's day at the mine, Gwak's first 72 hours. The workday gives the order. Learners meet the rules where they would meet them at work.

3. **Show the consequence, don't tell the rule.**
   - Gonzales: wrong choices bring consequences "of varying severity". In When Disaster Strikes she shows rather than tells.
   - Chea: a customer reply is triggered by each choice.
   - Wang: consequences for three different parties.
   - Ghiya: "outcome-based response" comes *before* "showing the correct response".

4. **A visible meter tied to a human outcome.**
   - Gonzales: a slider moves Milo toward adoption.
   - Gwak: consequence meters that move in two steps.
   - Chea: star rating.
   - The meter measures *the person you are helping*, not your points.

5. **A mentor or trainer you can ask, only when you want to.** Vernon (Chea), the veteran walker (Gonzales), the mentor (Wang, Chestnut), and the hint system after mistakes (Chestnut). It is help the learner pulls up, not lecturing pushed at them.

6. **Role choice at the start, same objectives, different scenarios.** Chea's Health & Safety does this exactly. The avatar choice in Gonzales is a lighter version.

7. **Real material from the SME.** Real customer emails (Chea), the SME's real lab accidents (Calvo), the outbreak story (Chestnut), and the error types of timing, route and measurement (Schwebel). Scenarios feel true because they are built from incidents.

8. **Push low-value content out to a job aid.** Calvo moved labeling to a job aid. Chestnut said a job aid alone won't change behaviour. In Chea's pilot, a learner kept the checklist at her workstation. The course drills the risky decisions, and the job aid holds the reference facts.

9. **One correct option plus two plausible ones.** Gonzales, Chea and Wang (3 × 3) all use this. Distractors are written from real mistakes.

10. **Retry with escalating help, then remediation that points back to a section.** Chea's asbestos course gives a retry, shows the answer on the 3rd attempt, and then a review table naming the section to revisit. Chestnut's hints appear after errors.

11. **Calm, purposeful visuals.** Hospital-calm colours with WCAG-checked contrast (Chestnut). A colour theme per section (Chea). Characters facing each other in a conflict (Wang). Narration rather than text walls (Adkison).
